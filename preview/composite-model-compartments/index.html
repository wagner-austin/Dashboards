How many compartments fit in one model

Austin Wagner

A serving design assumed several separately trained compartments could be wired into one request. Measuring it first put the naive limit at two — and then a training recipe moved four-compartment retention from −45.4% to +44.6%.

Question
Can one model hold several specialisations that stay distinct and stay separately addressable?
Result
Under naive training, no — past two compartments the primary's gain is erased. Train the base to expect a crowd and the limit moves.
Method
A sweep with its own artifact detectors, replicated across three roster rotations, with a bit-identity certificate: two separate processes produced SHA-256-identical records.
Stack
Python, PyTorch, a pretrained transformer, run on an RTX 3090 Ti offline.
Full record
The wiki pages below, pinned to the exact source blobs that produced the numbers.

The controls caught the result before a reader did

This is the part I would want to be asked about. The first roster read +27% retention at eight compartments — a good number, in the direction the design wanted, and wrong.

It was leakage. Compartments trained on two technical corpora were predicting a third corpus they had never seen, because all three share vocabulary (+0.18 and +0.41 cross-gain). The sweep scores every foreign compartment alone against the primary's held-out text precisely to catch that, so the artifact surfaced as a measurement rather than as a conclusion. The clean rerun put eight compartments at −7.0%.

Relatedness between compartments is measured, never assumed. Without that control the headline would have been the opposite of the truth, and nothing downstream would have contradicted it.

Moving the limit rather than reporting it

A ceiling is only a finding if you try to break it. The published literature names the escape route — concatenation works when it is trained for, not when it is assumed — so the next run trained the base to expect company instead of training each compartment alone.

At four compartments that took retention from −45.4% to +44.6%. The naive numbers stand as the baseline the recipe is judged against, which is the only reason the second result means anything.

What this does not settle

Every compartment measured here is a corpus. Whether the same curve holds when the compartments are dispositions — personas rather than bodies of knowledge — is open, and the published work suggests it is harder: the substrate that carries disposition degrades after very few composed directions, and the components a learned router selects track syntax and position rather than subject.

So a design that succeeds at composing corpora should not be assumed to transfer to composing characters. That is the next experiment, and it is deliberately unstarted rather than quietly skipped.

Record

Every figure above is bound to a named section of model-trainer-composition-ceiling and checked mechanically before this page can deploy. That page and model-trainer-companioned-training-recipe are pinned to the git blob hashes of the source files that produced the numbers, so a change to the measurement code invalidates the citation rather than silently outdating it. The survey of what the published literature permits is a separate page, many-personas-in-one-model-feasibility.

Known limits. One model family at one scale, measured offline on a single card; these are retention ratios on held-out text, not a serving benchmark, and nothing here times a request. The composition ladder belongs to a wider programme that has had published claims withdrawn for sitting below what its question set could resolve — the figures on this page are ratios with margins in the tens of points, not the near-ties that were withdrawn, and the withdrawal is recorded in the wiki rather than being left for a reader to discover.
